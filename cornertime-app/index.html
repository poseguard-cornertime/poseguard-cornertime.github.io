Session flow

A cornertime session, step by step

Every PoseGuard session goes through the same stages. The app speaks each instruction aloud, so the screen does not need to be visible during the session.

  1. Configure the session

    Choose an exact duration or a random time within a range. In random mode the real duration stays hidden and is revealed only after the session. Pick the face direction rule, sensitivity presets, penalties, and whether to record a timelapse.

  2. Device stabilization

    After pressing Start, PoseGuard reads the phone gyroscope and waits until the device has been completely still for 4 seconds. A moving camera would look like a moving body, so tracking never starts on a shaking phone.

  3. Take position

    A 10-second countdown gives time to step into the frame and take the pose. During the last 2 seconds the app calibrates which joints are clearly visible. If shoulders, elbows, hips, and knees are not all detected, the session does not start: “Camera cannot see the full body”.

  4. Reference pose is captured

    The current pose becomes the reference. The timer starts and the voice says “Time started. Hold your position”.

  5. Holding the pose

    Every camera frame is compared with the reference. Live Drift and Motion gauges at the bottom of the screen show how close the movement is to the threshold. Violations trigger a voice warning and, optionally, extra time on the timer.

  6. Victory or failure

    When the timer reaches zero the session ends in Victory. Reaching the maximum number of violations ends it in failure. The summary screen shows the initial and actual timer, violations by type, and offers to save the timelapse video.

Under the hood

The pose tracking pipeline

All processing runs on the Android device. The machine learning models are bundled inside the APK, so nothing is downloaded at runtime and no camera frames leave the phone. Each frame passes through these stages:

  1. CameraX1280×720 frames, only the newest kept
  2. Pose Landmarker33 body landmarks
  3. Identity checkfixes left/right swaps
  4. One Euro Filterremoves jitter
  5. Occlusion guardhandles hidden joints
  6. Movement trackerdrift and motion scores
  7. Session ruleswarnings and penalties

MediaPipe Pose Landmarker

Body tracking uses Google’s MediaPipe Pose Landmarker in live-stream mode. For each frame it returns 33 landmarks with x, y, z coordinates plus visibility and presence scores. PoseGuard tracks one person and requires 70% detection and presence confidence and 75% tracking confidence.

Two models are included: Heavy for best accuracy (default) and Full for faster processing on weaker devices.

GPU acceleration with CPU fallback

In Auto mode tracking starts on the CPU immediately, then the GPU delegate is initialized in the background and takes over once it is ready. If the GPU fails or does not initialize within 15 seconds, PoseGuard stays on the CPU and remembers the result for this device. GPU or CPU can also be selected manually.

Left/right identity stabilization

Pose models sometimes swap the left and right sides of the body, especially when the person faces away from the camera, which is common in cornertime. PoseGuard compares every new pose in both the direct and the mirrored form against the previous stable pose and keeps the closer one. Frames with impossible torso proportions are rejected as outliers.

One Euro Filter smoothing

Raw landmarks jitter slightly even when a person stands perfectly still. Each landmark is smoothed with a One Euro Filter: an adaptive low-pass filter that smooths heavily when the body is still and reacts quickly when it really moves. Without it, sensor noise would look like movement.

Occlusion guard

Hands behind the back or the head, or legs hidden behind each other, are hard for any pose model. During the last 2 seconds of the countdown PoseGuard measures visibility and jitter of elbows, wrists, knees, and ankles. Joints that are hidden or unstable are frozen at their position relative to the body, so a guessed position cannot cause false violations.

If a frozen joint becomes clearly visible for 5 frames in a row, tracking hands over to it smoothly over 8 frames.

Short dropout protection

If the detector briefly loses the body for up to 5 frames (180 ms), the last valid pose is held instead of reporting a disappearance. Longer gaps reset the filters so stale data is never compared with the reference.

Pose tracking

How movement is measured

PoseGuard compares 12 key points: shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, and ankles. Coordinates are centered on the hips and divided by the body size (the largest of shoulder width, hip width, and torso length). Because of this normalization the thresholds work the same whether the phone is 2 or 4 meters away.

Pose drift

Distance between the current pose and the reference. A violation is counted only if drift stays above the threshold for about 1.5 seconds, so a brief wobble is ignored but slowly changing the pose is not.

Sudden movement

Change between consecutive frames. Motion above the threshold for 0.9 seconds is a violation; a sharp spike of 2.5× the threshold counts immediately.

Sensitivity presets

Both thresholds are set as a fraction of body size. Wrists count at 70% weight in drift by default, because hands move slightly more than the rest of the body even in a strict pose. Small deadbands remove the remaining sensor noise before thresholds are checked.

Optional rule

Face direction

PoseGuard crops the head area using the face landmarks from the pose and runs MediaPipe Face Detector (BlazeFace short-range) on it. Choose “Face to camera” or “Face away from camera”. Breaking the rule for 5 frames in a row counts as a violation. If face detection stops working for 5 seconds, the session is stopped without a penalty.

Session timer

Timer penalties

Instead of ending the session after the first mistake, PoseGuard can add extra minutes to the timer. Penalties are set separately for the first, second, third, and every following violation.

Violations, penalties, and limits

PoseGuard counts four types of violations, each with its own voice warning:

Pose drift

“You changed pose”

Sudden movement

“You moved”

Face direction

“You looked at the camera” or “You turned away”

Out of frame

Key points missing for more than 1.5 seconds

Default penalty schedule

  1. 1st violation+1 min
  2. 2nd violation+3 min
  3. 3rd violation+3 min
  4. 4th violationSession failed

All values are configurable: penalties from 0 to 9999 minutes, the maximum number of violations from 0 to 9999, and the minimum interval between counted violations from 0 to 30 seconds (5 seconds by default), so one long movement is not counted several times. Penalties can also be turned off completely.

Feedback

Voice, sounds, and timelapse

Custom reactions for 10 events

Every event, from “Place the device still” to “Time is up” and each violation type, can use its own reaction: a Text-to-Speech voice with an editable phrase, your own audio file, a generated PCM tone (frequency, duration, channel, fade, single tone or double beep), phone vibration, or nothing.

Timelapse recording

PoseGuard can record the session as a timelapse: 10× speed, 30 fps, H.264 MP4. After the session the app asks whether to save it; saved videos go to Movies/PoseValidator on the device and are never uploaded.

Intiface Central (Online APK)

The Online APK connects to Intiface Central over WebSocket (ws://127.0.0.1:12345 by default) and controls compatible devices through buttplug.io. Vibration can run in the background while the pose is held and react to violations with a vibration or a pause, as a constant signal or pulses with adjustable strength.

Six languages

The app interface and voice prompts are available in English, Russian, Spanish, Italian, German, and French.

Phone setup

How to set up the phone

Place the Android device on a tripod or another stable support in portrait orientation. The gyroscope check will not start the session until the phone is still, and any camera shake during the session can look like body movement.

Choose a camera angle where the full body fits inside the frame with some margin. Remove extra objects that could block the view.

Good, even lighting helps the model see the body silhouette. Avoid dark rooms, strong backlight from a window behind the subject, and cluttered backgrounds when possible.

How to choose a pose that tracks well

PoseGuard can be used with different static poses, but tracking works best when important body points are visible. Shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, and ankles should not be heavily hidden behind the body or outside the frame.

Simple and clearly visible poses are usually more reliable than complex poses where arms or legs overlap heavily. Hands behind the head or back are supported by the occlusion guard, but the elbows should stay visible. Loose or bulky clothing makes pose tracking harder, while close-fitting clothing gives the app a clearer body shape.

If you get false violations, try a softer sensitivity preset, better lighting, moving the phone farther away, simplifying the pose, or switching to the Heavy model.

Who this app is for

PoseGuard is useful when a session needs more than a regular timer. It is made for stillness enforcement, posture training, pose-holding practice, cornertime, and consensual power exchange scenarios where a strict static pose should be held for a configured amount of time.

The app is currently under development, so behavior, default values, and available features may change between releases.
